Key Insights

* A total of **31 production batches** were analyzed, with an average production efficiency of **67.67%**.
* Total recorded downtime reached **1,388 minutes** across the analyzed production batches.
* **Operator-related issues accounted for 776 minutes (55.91%)** of total downtime, while non-operator-related causes accounted for **612 minutes (44.09%)**.
* The three largest downtime causes — **Machine Adjustment (332 min), Machine Failure (254 min), and Inventory Shortage (225 min)** — generated **811 minutes of downtime**, representing approximately **58.4% of total downtime**.
* Operator efficiency varied noticeably across the production line. **Charlie achieved the highest average efficiency at 70.97%**, while **Mac recorded the lowest at 63.26%**, resulting in a **7.71 percentage-point performance gap**.
* The average batch duration was approximately **103 minutes**, highlighting opportunities to reduce production losses through improved machine setup, maintenance planning, inventory availability, and operator-focused process improvements.

Business Implications

The analysis indicates that production losses are not driven by a single factor. More than half of total downtime is associated with operator-related issues, while machine adjustment, machine failure, and inventory shortages together account for the majority of recorded downtime.

Based on these findings, potential improvement areas include:

* Standardizing machine adjustment procedures to reduce setup-related downtime.
* Strengthening preventive maintenance practices to minimize machine failures.
* Improving inventory planning to prevent material shortages during production.
* Investigating operator-level performance differences and identifying opportunities for targeted training and process standardization.

These improvements could help increase production efficiency, reduce avoidable downtime, and create a more consistent production process.
